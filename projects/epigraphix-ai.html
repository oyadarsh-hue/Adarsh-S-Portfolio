01 / CONTEXT

The problem

Faint incisions, leaf fibres and irregular character boundaries make historical manuscripts difficult for ordinary OCR to read.

02 / RESPONSE

The approach

Combine image restoration, adaptive binarization, glyph segmentation and lexicon-based post-processing in an inspectable research workflow.

03 / UNDER THE HOOD

How it fits together.

  1. Fiber-aware processing and integral-image Sauvola thresholding separate script structure from the manuscript surface.
  2. The public Python recognition prototype combines geometric features, connected-component topology and simulated attention; trained-model inference is a separate validation step.
  3. Levenshtein alignment and a Malayalam trie lexicon support linguistic post-processing; ReportLab produces vector reports.

04 / CAPABILITIES

What it does

  • Adaptive glyph and word segmentation
  • Inspectable glyph-to-character prototype
  • Photometric surface inspection
  • Document-intelligence web studio

05 / ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

The important detail

Preserving small character loops and incisions while suppressing the leaf texture around them.

06 / OUTCOME

What came out of it.

A manuscript-analysis suite and an associated IJRASET research publication, published in August 2026.

Scope

The public Python engine simulates attention and maps features deterministically to characters. It does not demonstrate trained TrOCR inference or independently reproduced OCR accuracy.

Next engineering step
