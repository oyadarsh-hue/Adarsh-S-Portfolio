01 / CONTEXT

The problem

A shared photograph can expose people who never agreed to be visible. Identity and consent need to be considered before a face is revealed.

02 / RESPONSE

The approach

Detect faces, compare biometric embeddings with enrolled identities, and blur unconsented faces. The Veil workflow lifts the mask when the identified person grants permission.

03 / UNDER THE HOOD

How it fits together.

  1. Django modules separate enrollment, authentication, image processing and consent management.
  2. Dlib landmarks and ResNet facial embeddings support identity matching using Euclidean distance.
  3. MySQL stores application records; notifications connect consent requests to the social feed.

04 / CAPABILITIES

What it does

  • Privacy-by-default facial blurring
  • Biometric enrollment and verification
  • Consent-driven unblurring
  • OTP authorization and audit reporting

05 / ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

The important detail

Keeping recognition and user consent separate: a face match identifies a person, while permission determines whether the face can be shown.

06 / OUTCOME

What came out of it.

An integrated academic application connecting computer vision with a consent-aware social experience.
